MenuQuest Privacy Policy

Effective November 13, 2026

The short version: MenuQuest has no sign-up, ads, or tracking. Your restaurants, ratings, and notes stay on your device and in your own iCloud. A few things leave your device to make features work: menu photos you choose to scan (passed to Anthropic to read the menu), and restaurant searches and, if you allow it, your location (passed to Google to find restaurants). These go through MenuQuest's server, which knows your app only by a random ID and doesn't keep what you send. None of it is tied to your identity.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in MenuQuest — restaurants, menus, the dishes you've tried, ratings, notes, and saved menu photos — is stored on your device. If iCloud is turned on for MenuQuest, the same data syncs through your private iCloud database so it's backed up and available on your other devices. That data is stored by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the MenuQuest developer cannot access it.

What leaves your device, and why

Requests go to MenuQuest's server (hosted by Supabase), which passes them on to the service that does the work and sends back the result. The server doesn't store your photos, searches, or location.

DataSent toWhen and why
Menu photos, and the text your device reads from them MenuQuest's server → Anthropic (Claude API) Only when you tap “Read menu,” to turn the photos into a list of sections and dishes.
Restaurant search text MenuQuest's server → Google (Places API) Only when you search while adding a restaurant, to find and verify it.
Your current location MenuQuest's server → Google (Places API) Only if you allow location access, when you open “Add Restaurant,” to list restaurants near you. MenuQuest doesn't save your location or use it in the background.
The menu you scanned: section and dish names, descriptions, prices, and dish tags MenuQuest's server (Supabase) After you scan a verified restaurant's menu, so the next person who adds that restaurant gets the menu ready-made. Stored with the restaurant's Google Place ID and your random app ID (so bad uploads can be removed). Never includes your photos, ratings, notes, or which dishes you've tried, and isn't shown to other users with anything that identifies you.
Menu problem reports (the reason you pick and an optional note) MenuQuest's server (Supabase) Only if you report a problem with a shared menu, to fix or hide bad menus. Stored with your random app ID so each person counts once.
Names and descriptions of dishes on menus you've saved MenuQuest's server → Anthropic (Claude API) For dishes saved before MenuQuest started describing dishes, to describe them (cuisine, cooking style, flavors) so recommendations can use them. Your ratings and notes are not sent; recommendations are worked out on your device.
A random app ID, and how many scans and lookups you made today MenuQuest's server (Supabase) The first time you scan or look up a restaurant, the app gets a random ID with no name, email, or other details attached. The server counts uses per day to keep the service fair and affordable. The counts are only used for that.

These requests don't include your name or contact details. Anthropic and Google receive only the request itself, not your app ID. Everything is used only to provide the feature you asked for. Each provider handles the data under its own terms: Anthropic's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy; MenuQuest's server runs on Supabase (Supabase's privacy policy). Your photos, notes, and ratings are never shared with other users, and your notes and ratings never leave your device except through your own iCloud.

When you add a restaurant that has no menu in MenuQuest yet, MenuQuest's server may read that restaurant's own public website to find its menu, and adds it to the shared library. The only thing it uses from you is which restaurant you added.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No analytics SDKs.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.
  • No background location, and no access to your contacts or photo library beyond the photos you pick.

Location

MenuQuest asks for location access only “While Using the App,” and only uses it on the Add Restaurant screen to show places near you. You can say no and search by name instead, or change this anytime in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.

Camera and photos

MenuQuest asks for camera access so you can photograph menus, and lets you pick photos from your library. It only receives the photos you take or select.

Beta testing

If you test MenuQuest through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports and any feedback you choose to submit with the developer, as described in TestFlight's terms.

Deleting your data

You can delete any dish or restaurant in the app. Deleting the app removes its data from your device; to remove the iCloud copy, go to Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → MenuQuest → Delete Data.

Children

MenuQuest is not directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes — for example, if MenuQuest adds accounts or shared menus — the updated version will be posted here with a new effective date before the change ships.

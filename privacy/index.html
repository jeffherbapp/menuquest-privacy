MenuQuest Privacy Policy

Effective November 19, 2026

The short version: MenuQuest has no ads or tracking, and signing in is optional. Your restaurants, ratings, notes, trips and reservations stay on your device and in your own iCloud. A few things leave your device to make features work: menu photos you choose to scan (passed to Anthropic to read the menu), and restaurant searches and, if you allow it, your location (passed to Google to find restaurants). These go through MenuQuest's server, which knows your app only by a random ID and doesn't keep what you send. None of it is tied to your identity.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in MenuQuest — restaurants, menus, the dishes you've tried and the dates you had them, ratings, notes, quest progress, and saved menu photos — is stored on your device. MenuQuest keeps only the date of each meal, never the time or where you were. If iCloud is turned on for MenuQuest, the same data syncs through your private iCloud database so it's backed up and available on your other devices. That data is stored by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the MenuQuest developer cannot access it.

The same goes for your taste answers: the taste quiz, the allergies and diet you choose, foods you'll never order, and what you tell MenuQuest about dishes you rated. They're used on your device to pick dishes and flag ones that may not suit you, and sync through your private iCloud so they follow you to a new device. They're never sent to MenuQuest's server or to any AI service. You can change or reset them any time in Settings → Your tastes.

Trips you plan stay on your device and in your private iCloud too: the destination, the dates, the restaurant you plan for each meal, reservation times, and notes. Reservation reminders are notifications set on your iPhone; they aren't sent anywhere. Trip quests and must-go suggestions for your taste are worked out on your device. If you share a trip with other people, its plan is also kept on MenuQuest's server; see Shared trips.

Reservations you plan stay on your device and in your private iCloud too: the restaurant, the day and time, how it was booked (for example OpenTable, Resy or walk-in), whether it's confirmed, a booking link and your note. A reservation's reminder is a notification set on your iPhone; it isn't sent anywhere. MenuQuest doesn't book anything. The Dining Calendar is put together on your device from the meals you logged, your reservations, the tables you're at and your trips; nothing is copied anywhere for it. If you invite people to your table, that reservation is also kept on MenuQuest's server; see Shared tables.

Meal nudges (“Mark what you tried” and “How was it?”) are notifications set on your iPhone when MenuQuest sees you at one of your restaurants. The restaurant and when you were there stay on your device for a few hours to time them; they aren't sent anywhere. You can turn them off in Settings → Notifications.

What leaves your device, and why

Requests go to MenuQuest's server (hosted by Supabase), which passes them on to the service that does the work and sends back the result. The server doesn't store your photos, searches, location, or trip destinations (except trips and tables you choose to share; see Shared trips and Shared tables).

These requests don't include your name or contact details. Anthropic and Google receive only the request itself, not your app ID. Everything is used only to provide the feature you asked for. Each provider handles the data under its own terms: Anthropic's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy; MenuQuest's server runs on Supabase (Supabase's privacy policy). Your photos, ratings, and restaurant and dish notes are never shared with other users, and your ratings and those notes never leave your device except through your own iCloud.

When you add a restaurant that has no menu in MenuQuest yet, MenuQuest's server may read that restaurant's own public website to find its menu, and adds it to the shared library. The only thing it uses from you is which restaurant you added.

Your account (optional)

You can use MenuQuest without signing in. If you choose to sign in, with Apple or with a link sent to your email, MenuQuest's server (Supabase) stores your account's email address (with Sign in with Apple, this can be a private relay address Apple creates for you) and links your app ID to it. Your daily usage counts and any menus or menu reports you share are then linked to your account. Your restaurants, ratings, and notes still stay on your device and in your iCloud.

You can sign out or delete your account at any time in Settings › Account. Deleting it removes your account and email address from MenuQuest's server, along with any feedback you sent; menus you shared stay in the library but are no longer linked to you.

When we test a new feature before it's ready for everyone, MenuQuest's server keeps a testers list of the app IDs that can see it. If you're on one, your app ID is stored there until the feature is out or you're removed.

Shared trips

A trip stays private on your device and in your iCloud unless you share it. Sharing and joining a shared trip need a signed-in account.

When you share a trip, or join one from an invite link, MenuQuest's server (Supabase) keeps one copy of its plan so everyone on the trip can see and change it: the trip's name, the destination (its name and map location), the dates and time zone, each planned meal (the restaurant's name and Google Place ID, the reservation time and link, and the note), who planned or last changed each meal, and the people on the trip (their account ID, the name each person chose to show, and who owns the trip). Everyone on the trip sees all of this, including the names of the others. Up to 8 people can be on a trip.

If you allow notifications, MenuQuest can tell you when someone else on a shared trip plans, changes or clears a meal, or joins (at most one alert per person per trip every 10 minutes). For that, MenuQuest's server keeps your phone's push address from Apple with your account, and whether you want these alerts, and keeps a short log of each change (who, which meal and day, and the restaurant's name) for up to a week. Alerts go through Apple's push service and show the trip's name, the person's name and the change. You can turn them off in Settings › Notifications. Signing out removes your phone's push address; so does deleting your account.

Your reminders, ratings, the dishes you try, your notes on restaurants and dishes, and your trip quests are never part of a shared trip; they stay on your device and in your iCloud.

The invite link holds a code that lets anyone with it see whose trip it is, where, and when, and join it. The code is after the “#” in the link, so it isn't sent to the website. The owner can make a new link at any time, which stops the old one working.

The owner can remove people, make the trip view-only for guests, or stop sharing. Stopping sharing deletes the server's copy; everyone keeps their own copy on their device. Leaving a trip removes you from it. Deleting your account removes you from every shared trip and deletes the server's copy of the trips you own.

Shared tables

A reservation stays private on your device and in your iCloud unless you tap “Invite to my table”. Inviting people and joining a table need a signed-in account.

When you invite people to your table, or join one from an invite link, MenuQuest's server (Supabase) keeps one copy of it so everyone at the table can see it: the restaurant (its name, Google Place ID, address, map location and cuisine), the day, the reservation time and time zone, how it was booked, whether it's confirmed, the booking link, the host's note, and the people at the table (their account ID, the name each person chose to show, and who the host is). It also keeps the dishes people pin for the table (the dish's name, menu section and price, and who pinned it). Everyone at the table sees all of this, including the names of the others and who pinned each dish. Up to 8 people can be at a table.

Only the host can change the table's details. Table pins belong to the table only: they never change the dishes you've tried, your ratings or your pinned restaurants. Your reminders, ratings, the dishes you mark off, and your notes on restaurants and dishes are never part of a shared table; they stay on your device and in your iCloud. Reminders you set on a table you joined are notifications on your iPhone and aren't sent anywhere. Your phone keeps a copy of the tables you're at so they show without a connection.

The invite link holds a code that lets anyone with it see whose table it is, the restaurant, the day and time, and join it. The code is after the “#” in the link, so it isn't sent to the website. The host can make a new link at any time, which stops the old one working.

The host can remove people (their pins go with them) or stop sharing. Stopping sharing, or deleting the reservation, deletes the server's copy and its pins; the host keeps their own reservation. Leaving a table removes you and your pins from it. Deleting your account removes you from every table and deletes the server's copy of the tables you host.

The MenuQuest website

menuquest.app has no ads, analytics, or tracking cookies. If you request a beta invite there, we store the email address and first name you enter, the date, and that it came from the website, on MenuQuest's server (Supabase). We use them only to invite you to the beta and to tell you when MenuQuest launches. We don't share or sell them. To be removed, email us at the address below.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No analytics SDKs.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.
  • No background location, and no access to your contacts or photo library beyond the photos you pick.

Location

MenuQuest asks for location access only “While Using the App.” It uses it to show places near you on the Add Restaurant screen, to tell when you're at one of your saved restaurants, and to guess the restaurant you're at with Apple Maps. You can say no and search by name instead, or change this anytime in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.

Camera and photos

MenuQuest asks for camera access so you can photograph menus, and lets you pick photos from your library. It only receives the photos you take or select.

Beta testing

If you test MenuQuest through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports and any feedback you choose to submit with the developer, as described in TestFlight's terms.

Deleting your data

You can delete any dish, restaurant, trip or reservation in the app (deleting a trip also removes its meal plans and reminders; deleting a shared trip also leaves it, or stops sharing it if it's yours; deleting a shared reservation stops sharing its table), leave any table you joined, and reset your taste answers (here and in your iCloud) in Settings → Your tastes. Deleting the app removes its data from your device; to remove the iCloud copy, go to Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → MenuQuest → Delete Data.

Children

MenuQuest is not directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes — for example, when community ratings arrive — the updated version will be posted here with a new effective date before the change ships.
